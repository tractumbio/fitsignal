Exhibit 1

How the Department's data is structured

Three tables, chained by two foreign keys. region is the Department's own geographic grouping; every clinic sits in exactly one region; every clinic reports one row to annual_outcome per fiscal year it was open. Six years of collection means up to six rows per clinic — up to, because not every clinic has been open that long.

Six years of collection does not mean six years of data for every clinic. Three clinics opened after FY2019 — their earliest rows in annual_outcome start the year they opened, not FY2019.

Before you calculate anything

Three questions the data structure raises on its own

Worth asking the client

  • Is program_cost_usd clinical spend, outreach spend, or both? The table gives one number per clinic per year. If two clinics spend the same total but one is mostly outreach and one is mostly treatment, they aren't comparable on cost alone — and nothing here tells you which is which.
  • What's the denominator behind readmission_rate? Per patient enrolled, or per patient who completed the program? A clinic that lets struggling patients drop out before they're readmitted would show an artificially healthy rate under the second definition.
  • Does avg_a1c_reduction survive who leaves the program? It's a clinic-level average of whoever is still enrolled at measurement time, not a cohort followed from day one. A clinic with high dropout among its hardest cases can show an improving average for the same reason a fund's returns look better after its worst investors redeem.

None of that is in the schema — which is exactly why it has to be asked before Exhibit 2's numbers go anywhere near a recommendation.
